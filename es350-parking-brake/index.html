Building the Parking Brake From Scratch

Maya is back. The old parking brake parts got wrecked when the stuck rotor was cut off, so she is building the whole thing new from a bare plate. She has the DFC hardware kit and a box of DFC parking brake shoes. Yellow Grown-up tags need an adult. Purple Redneck hack tags are shop tricks that make the hard parts easier.

Do this after the new hub is bolted in and before the rotor goes on. The rotor guide is here: Putting the Back Brake Back Together.

How this brake works

A tiny drum inside the rotor

The middle of the rotor is shaped like a top hat. Inside that hat is a small drum. When you push the parking brake pedal, a cable pulls a lever. The lever pushes two curved shoes out against the inside of the drum, like pushing your hands out against the inside of a bucket so it can't spin.

Springs pull the shoes back in when you let go. The star wheel adjuster sets how close the shoes sit to the drum.

Not in your kit. Check you have these.

  • Parking brake shoe lever (the arm the cable hooks to)
  • Shoe strut (the flat bar between the shoes)
  • Shoe guide plate and its bolt on the anchor
  • Cable end and its spring, not frayed

If any got cut or bent, a Lexus or Toyota parts counter can sell them for 2007 to 2012 ES350. The 2007 to 2011 Camry parts match.

Sort the kit

The hardware kit and the 4 shoes are enough for both back wheels. Split everything in half. Half goes on this side. Put the other half back in the bag and write "PASSENGER" on it. Same walkthrough as a video

The steps, written out

Before step 1: pull the passenger side rear wheel, caliper and rotor and take photos and a short video of that parking brake. Then use your phone's photo editor to flip the photo sideways. Now it looks exactly like your driver side.

  1. Clean and grease the plate

    Wire brush the dust shield where the shoes ride. Spray it with brake cleaner. Find the small raised bumps (shoe pads) and put a tiny dab of high-temp brake grease on each one. Also a dab on the anchor where the shoe ends touch.

    Redneck hackUse a cotton swab for the grease. You get a dab the size of a pea every time, and your fingers stay clean for the shoes.

  2. Put the lever on the shoe Grown-up

    The lever goes on the shoe closest to where the cable comes through the plate. Push the lever's pin through the shoe hole. Slide the shim washer on, then the U-shaped C-washer. Squeeze the C-washer closed with pliers. The lever should swing freely but not wobble.

    Redneck hackClamp the shoe flat in a vise with rags on the jaws, or set it on a phone book. Then crimp the C-washer with Channellocks or vise grips.

  3. Hook the cable to the lever

    Make sure the parking brake pedal is all the way up (off). Pull back the little spring on the cable end and hook the cable into the slot at the bottom of the lever.

    Redneck hackSqueeze the cable spring back with needle-nose pliers, or slide a 10 mm open-end wrench over the cable to push the spring back while you hook it. If there's no slack, a grown-up loosens the cable adjuster under the car a few turns.

  4. Set the first shoe and lock it down

    Set the lever shoe on the plate. Push the hold-down pin through the back of the plate and through the shoe. On the front, stack it like a sandwich: cup, spring, cup. Push the top cup down and turn it a quarter turn so the flat pin end locks in the slot.

    It works like a childproof medicine cap. Push and twist.

    Redneck hackNo hold-down tool? Put the cup in a deep socket (one that just fits around it) on an extension and push and twist with that. Put a strip of tape over the pin head on the back side so it can't slide out while you work.

  5. Set the second shoe and lock it down

    Same thing on the other shoe. The kit has a straight pin and a bent pin for each side. The bent pin is for the hole that is blocked by the hub, so it reaches around. Test which one lines up before you load the spring.

    Redneck hackHold the bent pin from behind with locking pliers so it can't spin while you twist the cup on the front.

  6. Put in the strut and its spring

    The flat strut bar goes between the lever and the other shoe. Its small spring goes on the strut first. When the cable pulls, the lever pushes this bar, and the bar pushes the other shoe out.

    Redneck hackSqueeze the strut spring and hold it shut with a binder clip, put the strut in place, then pull the clip off.

  7. Put in the star wheel adjuster

    Screw the star wheel almost all the way in so the adjuster is short. Set its forked ends into the slots on the shoe tips, opposite the anchor. Hook the short green spring across the shoes next to it.

    Redneck hackPut a dab of anti-seize on the adjuster threads before it goes in. Next time it will still turn, not rust solid.

  8. Put the guide plate on the anchor Grown-up

    Set the guide plate back over the anchor, the way the passenger side has it. The Lexus factory manual says to put thread locker (Toyota 1344 or an equal) on the bolt threads. Tighten it to the factory spec.

  9. Hook on the two return springs

    These long springs pull the shoes back to the anchor. Hook one end into the shoe hole, then stretch the other end onto the anchor. This is the hardest step. Wear safety glasses because springs can fly.

    Redneck hackLoop a zip tie or a strong piece of string through the spring's hook, then pull the string with a screwdriver handle so your fingers stay out of the way. A bent coat hanger hook works too. Vise grips on the spring end give you a big handle.

  10. Do the wiggle test

    Push each shoe. Both should sit flat on the plate, move together, and spring back. Look at every spring next to your flipped passenger side photo. Count them. Nothing left in the tray except the other side's bag.

    Redneck hackPull the parking brake pedal once with the rotor off. You should see both shoes push out and come back. That proves the cable, lever and strut all work.

  11. Rotor on and adjust the shoes

    Slide the rotor over the shoes. Pull the rubber plug from the hole in the rotor, turn the rotor so you see the star wheel, and click it with a flat screwdriver until the rotor locks. Then back it off about 8 clicks so the rotor spins with just a light brush sound. Plug back in.

    Redneck hackPut 2 lug nuts on backward (flat side in) to hold the rotor tight against the hub while you adjust. Then the rotor can't wobble and fool you.

  12. Test and break in Grown-up

    After the caliper and wheel are back on, push the parking brake pedal and count the clicks. Compare to your owner's manual. To break in new shoes, Toyota says: drive about 30 mph, hold the pedal release button and press the parking brake lightly, keep going about a quarter mile, then let off. Do it 2 or 3 times in an empty lot.

More shop hacks

Cardboard map

Lay the passenger side parts on cardboard in the same shape they came off, or trace the shoes and draw where each spring goes. Now you have a puzzle picture to copy.

Muffin tin sort

One cup per kind of part. Egg cartons work too. Nothing rolls away under the car.

Build half on the bench

Some people hook the adjuster and its spring to both shoes on the bench, then open the shoes like a book and set them on the plate together.

Zip tie helper

Zip tie the shoes loosely around the hub to hold them in place while you hook springs. Snip it off before the rotor goes on.

Magnet on a stick

A telescoping magnet finds the cup or pin you dropped in the gravel. Cheap at any parts store.

Keep the shoes clean

Wear fresh gloves to handle shoes. Grease on the lining makes it slip. If it happens, scuff it with sandpaper and spray brake cleaner.

Paint marker on the star

Put a dot on one tooth of the star wheel. Easy to count clicks and turns when you adjust.

Phone video, not just photos

Film the passenger side while you take it apart. Play it backward and you see exactly how it goes back together.
